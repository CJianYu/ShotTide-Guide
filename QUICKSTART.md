# Your First ShotTide Storyboard

This guide follows the workflow described on the [ShotTide homepage](https://shottide.com/). It is a planning walkthrough; the example below is not a claim that a video has been generated or tested.

## 1. Write a small, concrete brief

Choose a subject and a short sequence before asking for a storyboard. For example:

> Plan a four-shot product story for a ceramic coffee mug. The audience is people who enjoy a quiet morning routine. Use soft window light and a warm kitchen setting. Begin with the mug on a table, show coffee being poured, move to a close-up of the handle, and finish with the mug beside a notebook. Avoid text overlays.

This is an illustrative creative brief, not an included template or a guaranteed output.

## 2. Review the shot plan

Open [AI Storyboard](https://shottide.com/ai-storyboard-generator) and start a project. Sign in when prompted by the application.

Review the proposed shots before generating media. Check:

- Whether every shot serves a distinct part of the story.
- Whether the subject, setting, and visual direction remain consistent.
- Whether the shot order makes sense.
- Whether prompts contain details you did not intend.

The public workflow supports editing prompts, arranging shots, and selecting versions. Review assistant suggestions before applying them.

## 3. Generate and compare

Choose image or video generation for the relevant scenes. Consult [pricing and credit costs](https://shottide.com/pricing) for the selected model before proceeding.

Compare the available results and select a version for each shot. A precise prompt helps communicate your intent, but AI output can still require revision. Check the actual media for continuity and visual errors.

If you already have a useful reference image, [Image to Video](https://shottide.com/image-to-video) is another starting point.

## 4. Add narration where it helps

Use [AI Voiceover](https://shottide.com/ai-voiceover) when the story benefits from spoken context. Keep narration concise enough to fit the intended sequence.

Before exporting, review the selected scenes and listen to the narration. Check names, pronunciation, pacing, and whether captions match what is spoken.

## 5. Export and review the file

The homepage describes MP4 export, with an accompanying captions file when voiced. Play the exported file and check both picture and sound before publishing it.

Inspect the first and last frames, transitions between shots, narration, and captions. Choose material you have permission to use, particularly when uploading reference images or depicting people.

## Continue exploring

For a sequence that needs recurring characters or multiple episodes, explore Drama Studio from the homepage. For composition planning, explore the 3D Shot Composer before spending credits on generation.
